# Deadlines: how each date is computed

**Verified as of 2026-10-08 against the Tax Code, Government Code and Comptroller pages listed in each row. Written for tax year 2027 (notices April 2027).** Every row is `verified-primary` unless the Rule column says it is practical advice. The table is meant to be mirrored by a script (`scripts/deadlines.py`); the prose after the table explains the judgment calls.

## Conventions for the table

- `T` is the tax year. Variables come from the user's documents: `notice_mail_date` (the date on the CAD's notice of appraised value, which is the mailing date), `hearing_date`, `first_hearing_day`, `order_received_date`, `arb_approval_date`, `delinquency_date`, `bill_mail_date`.
- `DATE(y,m,d)` builds a date. `d + Nd` and `d - Nd` count calendar days; the first day is excluded and the last day is included (Gov't Code §311.014(a)). `d + Ny` adds years. `MAX(a,b)` is the later date.
- `ROLL(d)` returns `d` if it is a business day, otherwise the next regular business day. A Saturday, Sunday, or legal state or national holiday on the last day moves the deadline forward (Tax Code §1.06; Gov't Code §311.014(b)). Memorial Day (last Monday in May) is a legal holiday (Gov't Code §662.003).
- Kinds: `deadline` = last allowed day; `latest` = latest day the other side may act; `earliest` = first allowed day; `window` = first..last allowed day; `advice` = not a legal deadline.
- **Safe-day rule for a script:** for deadlines counted backward from a hearing (`hearing_date - Nd`), print the statutory day and also the previous business day, and tell the user to act by the earlier one. Section 1.06 may excuse a next-business-day filing, but nobody should plan on it.
- Dates in the table are tied to the CAD or ARB documents the user actually holds. If the notice or order shows a different date than the rule yields, the user should follow the document and ask the ARB.

## Plain-English notes on the judgment calls

**The anchor is the date on the notice, not the date it shows up in the mailbox.** The statute counts 30 days from when the notice was "delivered." A first-class-mail notice is presumed delivered the day it is mailed (§1.07(c)), and the Comptroller's guidance reads the 30 days as running from mailing. The presumption can be rebutted with proof that the notice never arrived, but nobody should rely on that. Read the date off the notice and use it.

**May 15 usually wins for a homestead.** A homestead notice goes out by about April 1, and April 1 plus 30 days is May 1, so the May 15 floor controls. The 30-day rule only matters when a notice is mailed after about April 15, or for property that is not a homestead (notices by May 1). For 2027, May 15 falls on a Saturday, which moves the last day to Monday, May 17. CADs and ARBs often print their own date on the notice; if it is earlier than what the rule yields, protest by the earlier date.

**File early anyway.** Use the CAD's online filing where it exists and keep the confirmation. Mailed protests count if postmarked on or before the last day (§1.08); send them by a method that gives a dated receipt. Homestead owners in districts with an e-filing system can file, see the CAD's evidence, and take or refuse a settlement offer online (§41.415).

**Late protests have a ladder.**
1. Missed the deadline but the ARB has not yet approved the records: file at once and show good cause (§41.44(b)). The ARB decides what counts. The late-start window closes when the ARB approves the roll, usually by July 20.
2. Never got a required notice: protest the failure to give notice before the taxes are delinquent (§41.411, §41.44(c)).
3. Roll error found later: ask the ARB for a roll correction (§25.25). Clerical errors reach back 5 years; a big over-appraisal (more than one-fourth for a homestead) must be filed before the taxes are delinquent and costs a 10% penalty.
4. Exemption missed: late exemption application (2 years after delinquency for homestead, 5 years for a disabled veteran).

**Hearing-side clocks run backward from the hearing date.** Once the ARB mails the hearing notice (at least 15 days ahead), work back: the CAD owes its evidence and the pamphlet by 14 days before; the owner's certified appraisal or comparables to trigger a higher burden are also due 14 days before; ask for the CAD's data in writing as soon as the protest is filed so the 14-day cutoff in §41.67(d) works for you; request a single-member panel or a different hearing format in the notice of protest to avoid separate deadlines.

**After the order, the sixty-day windows start when you receive it.** The order comes by certified mail, or by e-mail if you elected electronic delivery. The statute counts from receipt, but the safest plan is to count from the order date or the mailing date and file well before. Pick only one route: filing in court waives binding arbitration (§41A.03(b)), and a SOAH appeal is an election of remedies (Gov't Code §2003.914).

**Pay the tax before the delinquency date, or the appeal dies.** Court: the lesser-of amount under §42.08(b). Arbitration and SOAH: the tax on the undisputed value. Delinquent taxes get an arbitration or SOAH appeal dismissed.

**What is not a legal deadline.** The usual ARB season (hearings from about May 15 to July 20), informal-hearing months, the length of hearing slots, and the number of printed copies are practice set by each ARB's published hearing procedures (§41.01(c), §41.66(a)). Read the county's procedures when they arrive.

**Law can still change before 2027.** The 90th Legislature convenes January 12, 2027, before the protest season. Re-verify this file after the session.
