# Hearing script: informal conference, ARB hearing, rebuttals, mock hearing

**Written for tax year 2027. Deadlines, statute cites and figures come from `law-and-figures.md`, `deadlines.md` and `beyond-arb.md` unless a line says otherwise (see Sources). Names, addresses and dollar amounts in the scripts are the fictional sample case (`examples/sample-case.json`); Claude replaces them with the user's case data. Rentals: use `income-approach.md` sections 4 and 6 for what to bring and income-specific counters.**

## 1. Preparation checklist, keyed to the evidence deadlines

H is the hearing date on the ARB's hearing notice. Dates counted back from H are in `deadlines.md`.

| When | Action | Source |
|---|---|---|
| At filing | Ask for the informal conference. Ask for a copy of the hearing procedures (box on Form 50-132). Request a single-member panel or a telephone or video hearing if you want one. Use Form 50-162 if someone else will attend for you | §41.445; §41.66(a); §41.45(b), (b-4); Form 50-132 |
| Right after filing, and no later than H - 21 days | Send the CAD a written request for the data, schedules, formulas and other information it will use. No form is required; keep proof of the date. Copies are free | §41.461(a)(2); the 21 days is advice, not a statutory deadline |
| H - 15 days | The ARB must have mailed the hearing notice. If you have none, call the ARB | §41.46(a) |
| H - 14 days | The CAD must deliver the Comptroller pamphlet, the hearing procedures and notice of your right to its evidence. Evidence you requested must arrive by now. If it does not, the CAD cannot use it except to rebut you, and the ARB must postpone once if you ask | §41.461; §41.67(d); §41.66(h) |
| 14 days before the first hearing day | Last day to file with the ARB and deliver to the chief appraiser a certified appraisal (CAD value $1 million or less) or comparable-sales, income and expense or unequal-appraisal information (if the CAD lowered the value last year, not by agreement). Either raises the CAD's burden to clear and convincing | §41.43(a-1) to (a-5) |
| H - 10 days | Hearing procedures copy, if you asked in writing. Single-member panel request, if not made in the notice of protest | §41.66(a); §41.45(b-4) |
| H - 5 days | Request a telephone or video hearing (H - 10 days if you have an agent). Evidence must then be by affidavit (Form 50-283) | §41.45(b), (b-1) |
| Before H | One postponement without showing cause. The ARB must answer a written request within 7 days; the new date is 5 to 30 days after the original unless the parties agree | §41.45(e); §41.66(n) |
| Start of hearing | Hand the CAD a copy of everything you will offer, before the hearing or immediately after it begins. An affidavit must reach the ARB before the hearing begins | §41.45(h), (i), (n) |
| After H | Missed the hearing: file a written statement of good cause asking for a new hearing within 4 days after H. The written order is due within 30 days after the hearing ends (45 in a county of 4,000,000 or more) | §41.45(e-1); §41.47(d), (f) |

If a computed day falls on a weekend or holiday, act by the business day before it. Example, H = Tuesday, June 15, 2027: the ARB notice is due May 31 (Memorial Day, so use Friday, May 28); request the CAD's evidence by May 25 (H - 21); the CAD's evidence and your certified appraisal are due June 1; the procedures copy and single-member request June 5 (Saturday, so act by Friday, June 4); the remote-hearing request June 10.

Before the hearing:

1. Compare the CAD's evidence with yours, comp by comp. Mark where it differs from your property.
2. Read the ARB's hearing procedures for copies, accepted electronic formats and any time limit. The statutes set none of these.
3. Set your target and floor (section 2) and tell Claude both.
4. Build a one-page summary: notice value, your opinion of value, three points, the numbers behind each.
5. Assemble the packet (section 3 list) and say the opening aloud once. If the user opted in, practice with the mock hearing (section 6).

Written request for the CAD's evidence:

> Under Tax Code section 41.461(a)(2), I request copies of the data, schedules, formulas and other information you will introduce at the hearing for account R000123456, including the comparable sales or appraisals used, the adjustments applied and their source, and any ratio study or sample used. Please deliver by e-mail.

## 2. Informal conference script

The CAD must hold the conference if you ask for it, and it happens before the ARB hearing (§41.445). The appraiser can offer a changed value. An agreement with the chief appraiser on a matter you could protest is final (§1.111(e)); a declined offer does not end the protest. Some districts send offers through an online portal; the same rules apply to accepting there.

**Set your numbers first.**
- **Target** = your argued value, for example $425,000.
- **Floor** = the highest value your own evidence supports, for example $432,000 (rounded up from the equalized value of $431,671, the highest indication in the case; the highest adjusted sale is $430,900). Do not set a floor above your best evidence; you could not defend it at the board.
- **Price the gap.** Savings = value reduction x total rate / 100. At the fictional 2.15 per $100, each $10,000 is $215 a year. $465,000 to $425,000 saves $860; to $432,000 saves about $710. The gap is about $150 a year. For a capped homestead count only the reduction below the capped amount (§23.23(a)).
- The ARB hearing has no fee (§41.41(d)) and the board cannot set a value above the CAD's records unless you ask and agree (§41.47(c-2)). The cost of declining is time. A value lowered by ARB order, arbitration or court carries into the next year (§23.01(e)). Whether an informal agreement does the same is not established here; ask whether the CAD will enter it as an agreed ARB order (§41.47(g)).

**Opening line.**

> Thank you for meeting with me. I am protesting the 2027 value of 123 Example Street, account R000123456. The notice says $465,000. My opinion of value as of January 1, 2027 is $425,000. I have three points.

**Three strongest points** (pick the three best-supported; one sentence each, then the number and the page):
1. Sales: comparable properties near mine, adjusted, have a median of $421,000 (page 1).
2. The district's own appraisals: mine is the second highest of nine homes at $221.43 per sq ft against a median of $205.56 (page 3).
3. Purchase or condition: I bought it on March 12, 2026 for $428,000 after 21 days on the market (closing disclosure, page 5).

Then ask: "What evidence supports $465,000 for this property: which sales, what adjustments, what sources?" Write the answer down.

**When you get an offer.**
1. Write the number down and say it back: "So the offer is $X."
2. Compare it with your floor before you answer.
3. Ask what moved the district: "What evidence moved you from $465,000 to $X, and what would move you to $425,000?"
4. If you counter, move once and give the reason, tied to a comp or a document. Do not split the difference.

| Outcome | Say | Next |
|---|---|---|
| Offer at or below your floor | "I accept $X. Please send me the agreement and I will read it before I sign." | Check value, tax year and account number on the paper. Sign only what matches |
| Offer above your floor, below the notice | "My sales support $425,000. Which of my comps did you discount, and why?" | One counter with a reason. If there is no movement, decline |
| Value stays at the notice value | "Please show me the evidence for $465,000: the sales, adjustments and sources." | If none, say you will go to the ARB hearing. Confirm in writing that your evidence request stands |
| Offer depends on an interior inspection | "I will consider it. Please put the request in writing." | Decide first; an inspection can find features the record lacks |
| The appraiser cannot agree to a number | "Who can? If no one, I will proceed to the hearing." | Note the name and date |

**Accept or decline.** Accept when the offer is at or below your floor and the paper matches what was said. Decline when it is above your floor and the appraiser could not answer your evidence or has shown none. Accepting above your floor is a legitimate choice if the yearly gap is smaller than the value of your time; make it on purpose and tell Claude. Declining leaves the ARB hearing in place.

## 3. Formal ARB hearing script

Facts: an ARB has at least 3 members and may sit in panels of 3 or more, or a single-member panel on your request; the full board decides from the panel's recommendation (§6.41; §41.45(b-4), (d), (d-2)). Testimony is under oath. You choose whether to present before or after the CAD; each side may offer evidence, question the other and argue (§41.66(b); §41.67(a)). The CAD must prove value by a preponderance of the evidence (§41.43(a)). The hearing starts at a set time; if it has not started within 2 hours you may require a postponement (§41.66(i)). ARBs must offer Saturday or after-5 p.m. weekday hearings, and owners who are 65 or older, disabled, military or veterans (and spouses) are scheduled ahead of agent-filed protests (§41.66(j-2); §41.71).

**Bring.** Notice of appraised value; your protest and any hearing notice; the CAD's evidence packet with your notes; the one-page summary; comp spreadsheet and unequal-appraisal pages; dated photographs, repair estimates and closing disclosure as they apply; copies as the ARB's procedures require (the statute requires a copy for the other side, §41.45(h); the ARB's set and format come from 34 TAC §9.805 and the ARB's procedures, §41.45(p); keep your own). Rentals: `income-approach.md` section 4.

**Steps.**
1. Check in early, or log in early for a remote hearing.
2. Choose the order. Going first lets you set the frame; going second lets you answer the CAD. Decide beforehand and be ready for either.
3. Hand over copies when the procedures say; no later than the start of the hearing (§41.45(h)).
4. Open (below), make your three points, then stop. Fit it to any time limit in the procedures.
5. Listen to the CAD. Take notes. Ask short questions: Is this sale arm's length? What adjustments, and from what source? Was it adjusted to January 1, 2027? Which of these did you deliver to me, and when?
6. If the CAD relies on material you requested and did not receive at least 14 days before H, say so and ask for a postponement or exclusion (§41.66(h); §41.67(d)). Other procedural failures: `beyond-arb.md` section 4 (written notice within 5 business days).
7. Close (below). Ask when to expect the written order.

**Opening.**

> My name is Pat Sample. I own 123 Example Street, account R000123456. I am protesting the 2027 value under Tax Code section 41.41(a)(1) and (a)(2). The notice value is $465,000. My opinion of value as of January 1, 2027 is $425,000. I will make three points.

**Closing.**

> To summarize: the district's value is $465,000. The adjusted sales, the district's own appraisals of my neighbors, and my purchase price support $425,000. I ask the board to set the 2027 value at $425,000. Thank you.

**After.** The order comes within 30 days of the hearing's end (45 in a county of 4,000,000 or more). If the result is not enough, the appeal clocks (60 days for arbitration and for court) start on receipt of the order: `beyond-arb.md` section 2.

## 4. Rebuttals to common CAD counters (homestead)

Say each reply in your own words, with your own numbers. Rentals: also use `income-approach.md` section 6; R1 to R3, R8, R9 and R11 apply to rentals too.

**R1. "Your comps are not comparable."**
Reply: "Which factor differs: size, age, condition or location? I adjusted for each, and the adjustments and sources are on page 2. Which sales does the district treat as comparable, and what adjustments did it apply?" Basis: §23.013(d); §23.01(f).

**R2. "The market is up."**
Reply: "The value date is January 1, 2027. My sales closed between May and December 2026 and each is adjusted to January 1. What data show a rise for homes like mine, and is it in the evidence I requested?" Basis: §23.01(a); §23.013(c).

**R3. "We use mass appraisal."**
Reply: "Each property is appraised on its own characteristics, and all available evidence specific to it must be considered. I am presenting evidence specific to this property." Basis: §23.01(b).

**R4. "Your purchase was not arm's length."**
Reply: "It was listed on the open market for 21 days with agents on both sides. The parties were unrelated and neither was under pressure. That is the definition of market value." Basis: §1.04(7). If the sale was related-party or under pressure, do not lead with it.

**R5. "Your price per square foot is average."**
Market value reply: "Neighborhood averages are not the test. The question is what this property would sell for on January 1, 2027, and adjusted sales say $421,000."
Unequal reply: "The test is the median, not the average. Mine is $221.43 against a median of $205.56, second highest of nine." Basis: §41.43(b). If the subject is at or below the median, drop the unequal claim.

**R6. "Neighbors' appraisals are not evidence of value."**
Reply: "They are not offered as evidence of sale price. The question is whether my property is appraised higher than comparable properties, which uses the district's own appraisals." Basis: §41.43(b)(3).

**R7. "Your neighbors have upgrades or better quality."**
Reply: "The district's records give the same quality and condition class. Show me the upgrade and I will adjust for it, using the district's own value for that item." Basis: §23.01(f).

**R8. "Online estimates and listings are not sales."**
Reply: "Agreed. My main evidence is closed sales. The estimates and the unsold listing at 14 Mock Orchard Road show market direction only." Do not lean on estimates or asking prices.

**R9. "Your sales are too old."**
Reply: "They closed within 24 months of January 1, 2027, and the window is 36 months for residential property in counties with more than 150,000 people. Each is adjusted for time." Basis: §23.013(b), (b-1), (c).

**R10. "Foreclosure or distressed sales cannot be used."**
Reply: "For a residence homestead the chief appraiser may not exclude comparable residential property in the same neighborhood only because it sold at foreclosure in the three years before the tax year, or because its value declined in a declining economy. I list it as supporting evidence." Basis: §23.01(c). A pressed seller does not meet §1.04(7)(C), so keep these as support.

**R11. "We have higher sales in your area."**
Reply: "Please show the list with addresses, dates and adjustments. I requested the district's evidence on May 20 and did not receive it 14 days before the hearing, so it cannot be used except to rebut. On the merits, how does each differ from my property?" Use the second sentence only if it is true. Basis: §41.461; §41.67(d).

**R12. "The 10% cap already protects you."**
Reply: "The cap limits the appraised value. The notice also shows a higher market value, and the cap closes the gap to it by up to 10% a year. A lower market value shortens that." Basis: §23.23(a), (b). If your argued value is above the capped amount, say there is no savings this year and decide whether to proceed.

**R13. "The condition problems are normal wear, or not documented."**
Reply: "Here are dated photographs from before January 1, the inspection report and two repair estimates totaling $38,000. Value is as of January 1 and depends on this property's own characteristics." Basis: §23.01(a), (b).

**R14. "Our record shows the living area and bath count we have on file."**
Reply: "The builder's floor plan and my own measurements, with photographs, show that the living area and bath count differ from the record. Please correct the record and re-run the value." Basis: §25.25(b); §41.41(a)(1). Think before offering an inspection.

**R15. "This is our best offer. The board may give you less."**
Reply: "I understand. The board cannot set a value above the district's records unless I ask and agree, and the district has the burden of proof. Unless we can reach $425,000, I will take it to the board." Basis: §41.47(c-2); §41.43(a). Say it only if the offer is above your floor.

## 5. Do and don't

**Do**
- State a number and a date: "my opinion of value as of January 1, 2027 is $425,000."
- Lead with your strongest point. Stay on three.
- Name each comp by address and say what you adjusted and why.
- Ask the CAD for the source of its sales, adjustments and ratios.
- Bring the closing disclosure, dated photographs and estimates that you cite.
- Read any agreement before signing it.
- Request a postponement before the hearing date if you cannot attend.

**Don't**
- Argue the tax rate, the budget or what you can afford. Those are not protest grounds under §41.41(a).
- Contact board members before the hearing. They may not discuss a protest outside the hearing (§41.66(f)).
- Present listings or online estimates as sales.
- Drop in comps you cannot explain, or hide ones that hurt you.
- State a statute you have not checked. Use the cites in this file.
- Agree to a value higher than the notice. The board cannot set one without your request and agreement (§41.47(c-2)).
- Skip the hearing without a postponement; you have 4 days after it to ask for a new hearing for good cause (§41.45(e-1)).

## 6. Mock hearing mode

Claude runs this only when the user opts in. Rules:

1. **Opt-in.** Offer it once at hand-off. If the user declines, do not ask again.
2. **Level.** Ask once: Easy, Standard or Hard (default Standard). Easy: 4 challenges, no follow-ups. Standard: 6 challenges, one follow-up when an answer is vague, ends with a counter-offer round. Hard: 8 challenges, follow-ups until the answer is specific, a counter-offer round, and one procedural question about evidence delivery (ask whether and when the user requested the CAD's evidence; do not claim the CAD failed to deliver it). If the user does not want a level, use Standard.
3. **Role.** Claude plays a CAD appraiser: plain, polite, no theatrics, no jokes.
4. **One challenge at a time.** State it in one to three sentences, name real addresses and numbers from the case, then wait. No hints, no multi-part questions, no coaching during the round.
5. **Source of challenges.** Pick from R1 to R15 the ones the case data supports. Skip any that needs a fact the case does not have (for example skip R4 when there is no purchase price). Pose R11 and R15 as conditional questions ("If the district has higher sales, which are they and how do they differ from mine?"), because the CAD's real evidence and offer are unknown. For a rental, use `income-approach.md` section 6 plus R1 to R3, R8, R9 and R11.
6. **Never invent.** Use only `case.json` and what the user said in the session. Do not make up comps, sales, defects, CAD evidence or appraiser authority. If asked what the CAD would really say, answer that this is practice and the CAD's actual evidence is unknown until it is delivered.
7. **Law.** The appraiser challenges facts and evidence, not citations. Any legal statement Claude makes must match `law-and-figures.md`. If the user cites a rule wrongly, correct it in the debrief, not during the round.
8. **Counter-offer round (Standard and Hard).** Offer the midpoint between the CAD market value on the notice and the user's argued value, rounded to the nearest $1,000. Say it is a practice figure, not a prediction. For a capped homestead, also say what that offer would save in tax: nothing if it is at or above the taxable (capped) value. Ask the user to accept, counter or decline against the floor they set in section 2. If no floor is set, ask for one first.
9. **No scoring.** No grades, no win or lose, no odds.
10. **User control.** "Pause", "stop", "skip" or "repeat" is honored at once.
11. **Debrief.** End with exactly three improvements, numbered. Each names the answer that prompted it and one concrete change: a document to add, a sentence to rewrite, a number to prepare. If the user stops early, still give three based on what was answered. Then ask whether to update `arguments[]` or `argued_value` in `case.json`; change nothing without a yes.
