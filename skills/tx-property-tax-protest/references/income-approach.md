# Rental path: income approach and the non-homestead cap

Researched 2026-10-08 for tax year 2027 protests (notices about April-May 2027). Load this file when the user picks the rental / investment path.

Scope: single-family rentals and small residential rentals (duplex, triplex, fourplex). Apartments (5+ units) and commercial get short notes only. Out of scope: portfolios, business personal property beyond what a landlord needs, short-term/vacation rentals.

Confidence labels used below:
- **verified-primary**: read in this research session in the statute, the Constitution, a Comptroller publication, or the appraisal district's own document.
- **secondary-only**: from news, consultants, lenders or search summaries; not confirmed in a primary source.
- **inference**: reasoning from primary text; no source states it.

How statute text was read: from the Legislature's file host (`tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/TX/htm/TX.NN.htm`), which backs statutes.capitol.texas.gov. File timestamps: Tax Code chapters 23 and 41 on 2026-04-10, Constitution on 2026-07-31. A law enacted after those dates would not show there. Re-check before relying on this file after January 2027.

## Bottom line

1. **The 20% non-homestead cap ("circuit breaker", Tax Code §23.231) is written to end on Dec 31, 2026, and so is its constitutional authority.** Nothing found extends it. For tax year 2027, plan on **no cap**: the taxable appraised value is the market value, so market value is the number to protest. Confidence: high that it is not in effect under current law; medium that it will not be revived in time (that depends on the 90th Legislature, which has not met).
2. **For a single-family rental the CAD values by sales comparison.** The income approach is a supporting exhibit there. For duplex to fourplex it can be the main exhibit, and for 5+ units and commercial it is the CAD's own method.
3. **Income and expense data you give the CAD is confidential from the public (§22.27) but not from the CAD's appraisers.** Some CADs say they build their income schedules from owner-supplied data, including data given during protests.

## 2. When to use the income approach vs sales comps

What the sources say (Section 1, B2 to B8): the CAD must consider all three methods and use the most appropriate. The Comptroller says sales comparison is typically preferred for single-family homes. Comal applies income schedules to duplexes through fourplexes and builds none for single-family houses; Tarrant uses income for low-density multifamily only when sales are lacking; Tarrant and Harris run income models for apartments and other commercial income property.

| Situation | Lead with | Role of the income approach | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Single-family rental, plenty of neighborhood sales | Sales comps (and unequal appraisal; see `unequal-appraisal.md`) | Supporting only: shows condition or rent problems, or a GRM sanity check | Comptroller and CADs favor sales here; Comal builds no income values for single-family |
| Duplex, triplex or fourplex with few good sales | Income approach + GRM, plus the best sales available | Main exhibit | Tarrant allows income when recent sales are lacking; Comal applies income schedules to these |
| Duplex to fourplex where the CAD used an income schedule | Rebut each input (rent, vacancy, expenses, cap rate) | Main exhibit | Ask for the schedule (§41.461) and compare line by line |
| 5+ units or commercial | Income approach, with full financials | Main exhibit | It is the CAD's own method (B5, B7, B8). Consider a property tax professional |
| Your rent is below market by choice (family tenant, long-term discount) | Market rent, not actual rent | Use market rent or skip | CAD analyzes comparable rents or earning capacity (§23.012(a)(1)); whether a CAD accepts below-market contract rent is unverified (Section 7) |
| Real condition problems (long vacancy, major deferred repairs) | Condition evidence and adjusted sales | Show the lost rent and repair quotes | §23.01(b) individual characteristics |
| Bought recently at arm's length | Closing disclosure first (see `strategies.md`) | Support | A recent arm's-length price fits the §1.04(7) definition; its weight is covered in `strategies.md` |
| Your honest numbers give a value above the CAD's | Do not lead with income | Skip it, use sales or unequal appraisal | The ARB cannot raise the value (§41.47(c-2)), but the CAD may keep and reuse your data later (inference from §22.27(a) and D6) |
| Rent-restricted or low-income housing owned by an organization | Statute directs the income method (§23.215) | Ask the CAD | Out of scope |

Honest limits of the income approach for a small rental:
- It rests on three judgment inputs: market rent, expenses and cap rate. The CAD can challenge each one.
- In the Section 3 example, moving the loaded cap rate by one point moves the value from about -10% to +12.5%. An unsupported cap rate is the easiest thing for the CAD to discount.
- Many buyers of single-family houses are owner-occupants who do not price off rent (general appraisal reasoning, not a cited fact), which is one reason the CAD and Comptroller lean on sales.
- Income value and sales value should land near each other. If they are far apart, find out why before the hearing; the CAD will ask.
- Texas §23.013(d) lists income, operating expenses and occupancy as comparability factors. That is the strongest statutory hook for saying a CAD's sales comps are not comparable to a tenant-occupied rental.

## 3. Step-by-step method

Valuation date is Jan 1 (§23.01(a)). For tax year 2027 use calendar-year 2026 actuals, the rent roll as of Jan 1, 2027, and market-rent evidence for that date.

1. **Gross potential rent (GPR)** = sum of market rent for every unit x 12. Use what a typical tenant would pay now (at least 3 rent comps: listings, signed leases of similar units, a property manager's letter). If actual rent is lower, say why.
2. **Vacancy and collection loss** = GPR x vacancy %. Use multi-year history and market vacancy. Use a stabilized figure, not your worst month. Do not count turnover cost here (it goes in expenses).
3. **Other income** = pet fees, late fees actually collected, laundry, parking. Leave out security deposits and one-time items.
4. **Effective gross income (EGI)** = GPR - vacancy and collection loss + other income.
5. **Operating expenses** (recurring costs of running the property):
   - include: property management at a market fee (CADs assume prudent management, so include it even if you self-manage), insurance, repairs and maintenance, owner-paid utilities, landscaping, pest and trash, HOA dues paid by the owner, leasing and turnover costs, replacement reserves, and property tax (see step 8)
   - leave out: mortgage principal and interest, depreciation, income taxes, capital improvements, personal or travel costs (the Nueces CAD excludes "capital expenditures, debt service, depreciation, travel")
6. **Net operating income (NOI)** = EGI - operating expenses.
7. **Capitalization rate**: sourcing below.
8. **Value = NOI / cap rate.** Property tax depends on the value you are solving for, so use one of two methods and not both: (a) put an estimated tax in expenses and re-solve until the tax matches the value; or (b) use NOI before property tax and a tax-loaded cap rate: value = NOI before tax / (cap rate + effective tax rate). This is general appraisal practice; ask the CAD which it uses (Section 5 request).
9. **Gross rent multiplier (GRM) cross-check**: GRM = sale price / gross annual rent of a sold comparable. Value = median GRM x subject's gross annual rent. Use rent on the same basis (market or actual) for the comps and the subject, and use only comps with similar expense structure and tenancy, because GRM ignores expenses. TAD and HCAD list "income multipliers" among accepted tools (B8).
10. **Reconcile** with sales comparison and give one opinion of value with reasons. Round it.
11. **Savings** = (CAD taxable value - argued value) x total tax rate on the CAD record. Rentals have no homestead exemption. For TY2024-2026 the starting point was the capped value, so savings counted only below it; for TY2027 expect the market value to be the starting point (A12).

**Where to source a cap rate (no numbers are given here on purpose).** Rank by credibility:
1. **Derived from closed sales of similar small rentals** (price, in-place rent, actual expenses). Best evidence, and it is how Tarrant and Harris say they derive theirs (B8). Sale prices are not part of the public deed record in Texas (general knowledge, not verified here), so data usually comes from an agent's MLS "residential income" sales, a broker, or the seller/buyer.
2. **The CAD's own rate and its source**, requested under §41.461. §23.012(a)(3) requires the CAD to analyze comparable data for its rate.
3. **Band of investment** (weighted mortgage rate and equity return), which Tarrant and Harris also use. Weak unless the inputs are documented.
4. **Broker opinion of value or a short appraisal** from someone who sells or appraises small multifamily locally.
5. **Published surveys, as context only.** CBRE's U.S. Cap Rate Survey (H1 2026, published 2026-08-12; covers 50+ markets and sectors including multifamily; free-download terms not confirmed) targets institutional-grade property, not 1-4 unit rentals. The Texas A&M Real Estate Research Center's cap-rate study (publication 2265) covers four metros through Q3 2019 and is out of date. Neither gives a small-rental rate.
6. **Avoid**: calculator pages and lender marketing pages (several show identical tables across cities), and listing-site cap rates, which are asking-price rates on seller pro forma numbers.

### Worked example (ILLUSTRATION ONLY)

Fictional duplex at 100 Example Lane, Sampletown, TX. Every number below is invented to show the arithmetic. None is market data, and none should be used as a rate or rent for a real property.

| Line | Amount |
|---|---|
| Market rent, 2 units x $1,500 x 12 = GPR | $36,000 |
| Vacancy and collection loss, 6% of GPR | -$2,160 |
| Other income (pet fees) | +$600 |
| **EGI** | **$34,440** |
| Property management (about 8% of EGI) | $2,750 |
| Insurance | $1,800 |
| Repairs and maintenance | $2,400 |
| Landscaping, pest, trash, owner-paid utilities | $900 |
| Leasing and turnover | $700 |
| Replacement reserves | $1,000 |
| Operating expenses before property tax | $9,550 |
| **NOI before property tax** | **$24,890** |
| Cap rate 7.00% + effective tax rate 2.00% = loaded rate | 9.00% |
| **Income value** = $24,890 / 0.09 | **$276,556, about $276,600** |
| Check: tax at that value 2.00% = $5,532; NOI after tax $19,358; $19,358 / $276,600 | 7.0% |

Sensitivity (same NOI): loaded rate 8% gives $311,125 (+12.5%); 10% gives $248,900 (-10.0%).

GRM cross-check with three fictional sold duplexes:

| Comp | Sale price | Gross annual rent | GRM |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | $265,000 | $34,800 | 7.61 |
| B | $300,000 | $39,000 | 7.69 |
| C | $280,000 | $37,200 | 7.53 |

Median GRM 7.61 x $36,000 = about $274,000. That sits within 1% of the $276,600 income value, so the two checks agree.

Reconciliation in the example: the fictional CAD value is $340,000 and its sales comps indicate about $335,000, but those comps are owner-occupied homes in better condition. Income and GRM point to $274,000 to $277,000. The owner argues $280,000, rounded up above both indications. At a fictional 2.0% total tax rate the reduction of $60,000 saves about $1,200 a year. Note that even the high end of the sensitivity range ($311,125) is below the CAD value, but the more the argued value rests on the cap rate, the more the cap-rate source must be documented.

## 4. Evidence checklist and what to bring

Timeline for tax year 2027 (check `deadlines.md` for the authoritative dates): rendition, if required, by April 15, 2027 (written extension to May 15); notice of appraised value by about May 1; protest deadline the later of May 15 or 30 days after delivery of the notice (May 15 is a Saturday in 2027, so May 17); ask for the CAD's evidence right after filing; deliver your own evidence at least 14 days before the hearing if you want the §41.43(a-1) or (a-3) burden shift.

| Bring | Why it matters | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Notice of appraised value, showing market value and (through TY2026) the capped value | Shows what is being protested and the cap gap | For TY2027 expect one value |
| Notice of protest (Form 50-132) and, if used, agent form 50-162 | Standing | https://comptroller.texas.gov/forms/50-132.pdf |
| Income and expense statement, 12 months of the prior year, month by month | The core of the income approach. CADs ask for it (Nueces CAD asks for "all twelve months of the preceding year") | Operating items only; strip debt service, depreciation, capex |
| Rent roll as of Jan 1: unit, size, rent, other income, lease start and end | Shows GPR and occupancy on the valuation date | Nueces CAD list also wants annual rent per unit |
| Leases | Backs the rent roll | Redact tenant names and IDs if not needed |
| Vacancy log (dates vacant, days marketed, reason) and unpaid-rent records | Backs the vacancy and collection-loss input | |
| Expense proof: invoices, insurance declaration page, management agreement, utility and HOA statements, property tax bills | Backs each expense line | Highlight one-time items you removed |
| Schedule E (Form 1040) for the prior year, reconciled to your statement | Independent tie-out | Schedule E includes depreciation and interest; show the reconciliation |
| Capital items list (roof, HVAC, etc.) kept separate | Shows you did not mix capex into expenses | Supports reserves |
| Market-rent evidence: 3+ comparable rentals (listings, leases, manager letter) | Supports GPR | Same unit type and area, near Jan 1 |
| Sold-rental data for cap rate and GRM: price, rent, expenses, date | Supports the rate and the multiplier | Show your derivation sheet |
| Sales comps (see `strategies.md`) | The CAD will use sales; you must answer them | Explain differences in occupancy, income and condition (§23.013(d)) |
| Photos and repair estimates | Condition and functional issues | Dated photos |
| Purchase documents if you bought in the last 2 years (closing disclosure, inspection report, any appraisal) | Evidence of market value | |
| The CAD's evidence packet (requested under §41.461) | So you can rebut it | Mark where its inputs differ from yours |
| One-page summary: your value, the three inputs, the two cross-checks | Keeps a short hearing on track | |
| Copies | Local ARB procedures state how many copies and any accepted portable-device format (Comptroller Rule 9.805 requires each ARB to say) | Bring extra |

Informal conference: bring one organized set; the appraiser may adjust the value on the spot. ARB hearing: the panel hears both sides; use the same package, lead with the three inputs and the strongest cross-check, and ask for the order you prefer (owner may go first or second, §41.66(b)).

For 5+ units and commercial property: the CAD already runs an income model with its own rent, vacancy, expense and cap-rate inputs. Expect to supply full financials and to argue each input. Consider a property tax professional.

## 5. Confidentiality and requesting the CAD's evidence

**What is protected (§22.27)**
- Your income and expense information, rent rolls, and attachments are not open to public inspection.
- The CAD may disclose them to you, to the Comptroller, in a tax proceeding where you are a party, and in statistical form that does not identify the property or owner (D2). That last exception means your numbers can shape the CAD's market schedules without your name.
- Other owners' rent rolls are protected the same way, so expect schedules and statistics from the CAD, not identified comparables' financials.
- Ask for confidentiality in writing when you submit. Nueces CAD says it will hold the documents confidential on request and cites §22.27. A confidentiality stamp on your pages is good practice.

**Hearings are open**
- ARB hearings are open to the public. If you will show a rent roll or financials, ask the CAD representative to join a motion for a closed hearing (§41.66(d-1)). The statute requires a joint motion, so the CAD must agree to file it.
- Until a closed hearing is granted, assume anything said or shown in an open hearing can become public (cautious reading, not stated by a source).

**Request the CAD's evidence (§41.461)**
- File the request in writing once your protest is on file. The CAD must send the pamphlet, the ARB procedures and a notice of this right at least 14 days before the hearing.
- Copies are free. If the CAD does not deliver requested items 14+ days before the hearing, it cannot use them except to rebut you (§41.67(d)), and you can ask the ARB to postpone once (§41.66(h)).
- If the CAD ignores the 14-day rule and the ARB will not enforce it, the Comptroller describes limited binding arbitration (Tax Code ch. 41A) for procedural failures; see `beyond-arb.md`. Not verified in this research (secondary-only, from a search summary of the Comptroller's page), and it has short notice deadlines.

Sample request (fill in the placeholders; use your own account number):

> Under Tax Code §41.461(a)(2), I request copies of the data, schedules, formulas and other information you will introduce at the hearing for account [CAD account number], including: the valuation method used; the market rent, vacancy and collection loss, other income, expense amounts or ratios, and capitalization rate or multiplier applied to this property, with the source of each; any income or expense information the district holds on this property; and the comparable sales, with adjustments and sale dates.

## 6. Common counters and suggested responses

| CAD counter | Suggested response | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| "Our sales comparison model supports the value." | Ask for the comps and adjustments. Point out where they are owner-occupied, in better condition, or have different income and occupancy; the statute makes income, operating expenses and occupancy comparability factors | §23.013(d); §23.01(b); §41.461 |
| "Investors and owner-occupants buy the same houses, so rent does not matter." | Duplex-to-fourplex buyers are usually investors (general reasoning). Show closed rental sales and the price-to-rent relationship. For a single-family house, concede that sales lead and use income as a cross-check | §1.04(7); §23.0101 |
| "We use market rent, not your actual rent." | Show 3+ rent comps that match your rent. If your rent is below market by choice, use market rent and do not argue the lower figure | §23.012(a)(1) |
| "Your expenses are not typical; we use a standard ratio." | Show invoices and a month-by-month statement. Ask for the CAD's ratio and its source. Include a management fee and reserves so your figures look like a prudent-manager model | §23.012(a)(2), (a)(4); B8 |
| "Your cap rate is too high or unsupported." | Show the derivation from closed sales with income data. Ask for the CAD's rate and how it was derived. Do not offer a rate with no source | §23.012(a)(3) |
| "We have no income data on comparables, so income cannot be used." | Then the CAD is on sales comparison; show why its sales are not comparable, and use GRM from sold rentals as a cross-check | §23.0101 |
| "Your figures are self-reported." | Tie out with leases, bank statements, invoices and Schedule E. Offer the originals | §41.67(b) |
| "The 20% cap already limits your increase, so there is nothing to protest." | The cap limits appraised value only, and only through 2026. Market value is the base, and for 2027 the cap is gone. A 2026 reduction also helps next year: if the CAD lowered your value in the preceding year (and not by written agreement under §1.111(e)), it carries the heavier clear-and-convincing burden when you deliver income and expense statements or comps 14+ days ahead | §23.231(k); §41.43(a-3) |
| "Recent sales are within 24 (or 36) months, so they are valid." | Ask for time adjustments, the market direction since each sale, and proof each sale was arm's length | §23.013(b), (b-1), (c) for the window and time adjustment; §1.04(7) for arm's length |
| "You did not file a rendition or income report, so you carry the burden." | Check whether one was actually required. Below the personal-property threshold none is. If the CAD sent a written request, answer it, and send a written penalty-waiver request with documents (later of June 1 or 30 days after the penalty notice) | §22.01(j-1); §41.43(d); §22.30(a) |
| "Your neighbors' rentals are appraised about the same." | Switch to unequal appraisal if the CAD's own appraisals of comparable properties are lower per unit or per square foot | §41.43(b); `unequal-appraisal.md` |

Common owner mistakes to remove before the hearing: mortgage interest or principal in expenses; depreciation; capital improvements in expenses; using one unusually bad month as the vacancy rate; leaving out a management fee; counting both a property tax expense and a tax-loaded cap rate; using asking rents that never leased.

## 7. Unresolved / needs human check

1. **Circuit breaker revival.** The 90th Legislature starts Jan 12, 2027. Prefiled bills usually appear in November 2026. Re-check capitol.texas.gov for any bill or joint resolution that touches Tax Code §23.231 or art. VIII §1(n) before the season, and again in April and May 2027. If anything passes, this file's A1 to A13 need rework. The skill should ask the user to confirm the notice shows one value or two.
2. **A called session after April 2026.** The Tax Code file is dated 2026-04-10. The Legislature Online session list showed no 3rd called session on 2026-10-08, but I did not search for a later special session announcement outside that list.
3. **Mixed-use homestead and rental** (owner lives in one unit of a duplex): how the CAD splits the homestead cap and the circuit breaker is not covered here. Ask the CAD. Moot if the cap lapses.
4. **Is a CAD's income-and-expense form "required" under §22.01(b)?** This affects the 10% penalty and the §41.43(d) burden shift. Text verified; each CAD's practice is not. Ask each CAD; the counties file (`counties.md`) should record it.
5. **Below-market contract rent.** I found no primary source saying whether a Texas CAD must use market rent or may use actual contract rent for a small rental. Harris says its Commercial Property Division appraises the fee simple interest but considers the effect of "leases, contracts" case by case (2026 report, PDF p. 35), so a lease argument is possible but up to the CAD. The skill should treat market rent as the safe basis.
6. **Which CADs use income schedules for duplex to fourplex.** Only Tarrant, Comal and Harris were read. The 25-county research should ask each CAD.
7. **Cap-rate data for 1-4 unit Texas rentals.** I found no free, authoritative source. The CBRE survey tables were not reviewed (landing page only); the Real Estate Center series ends in 2019. The skill must not suggest a number.
8. **Evidence format rules** (copies, portable devices) differ by ARB under Comptroller Rule 9.805. Check each county's adopted hearing procedures.
9. **Short-term rentals** (furnished, hotel-occupancy-taxed) were not researched.
10. **Statistics on how often income evidence wins** for small rentals were not found. This file makes no claim about win rates.
