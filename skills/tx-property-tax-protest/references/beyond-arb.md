# Beyond the ARB: arbitration, SOAH and court

**Verified as of 2026-10-08.** Sources: Tax Code ch. 41A, ch. 42, §25.25, §31.02; Government Code ch. 2003 subch. Z; Comptroller and SOAH web pages. URLs are in "Sources" at the end. Dollar figures and deadlines below are the ones in force for the 2027 protest season unless noted. The 2025 session changed three things that touch a homeowner appeal: refund timing and interest (SB 850), the split-payment cross-reference in §42.08 (HB 2742), and court discovery limits (HB 1533, §42.23(j)-(k)). Its other changes to ch. 41A (lessee arbitration, arbitrator continuing education) do not affect an owner's appeal.

## 1. Which route fits

| Route | Who can use it | Start by | Money to start | Who decides | Final? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Regular binding arbitration** (Tax Code ch. 41A) | Owner of a **residence homestead of any value**, or other property whose ARB-ordered value is **$5 million or less**, after an ARB order on a **value or unequal-appraisal** protest | **60 days** after you receive the ARB order | Deposit: **$450** (homestead valued at $500,000 or less), **$500** (homestead over $500,000) | An arbitrator the Comptroller appoints | Yes. The award is binding and generally cannot be appealed. |
| **Limited binding arbitration** (§41A.015) | Owner who filed a protest and believes the ARB or chief appraiser broke a listed **procedural rule** | Certified-mail notice within 5 business days of the violation, then file on days 11-30 after that notice | Deposit: **$450** (homestead valued at $500,000 or less), **$550** (other) | A licensed-attorney arbitrator | Yes, but it only orders compliance or a new hearing. You can still appeal the value. |
| **SOAH** (Gov't Code ch. 2003, subch. Z) | Real or personal property **not industrial**, where the ARB value is **over $1 million** | Notice of appeal **30 days** after you receive the order; deposit within **90 days** | **$1,500** deposit | An administrative law judge | Trial de novo before the ALJ; an election of remedies against court |
| **District court** (Tax Code ch. 42) | Any owner who got an ARB order, for any protest ground | **60 days** after you receive notice of the final order | Court costs vary by county; a lawyer is usually needed | A judge or jury, trial de novo | Appealable like other civil judgments |

Binding arbitration and SOAH are for value and unequal-appraisal disputes only (§41.41(a)(1), (a)(2)). **If the ARB upheld a denial of an exemption, arbitration and SOAH are not available; the remedy is district court** (§42.01(a)(1)(A)).

You pick one. Filing in court waives binding arbitration on that value (§41A.03(b)); a SOAH appeal is an election against a court appeal (Gov't Code §2003.914).

## 2. First ten minutes after the ARB order arrives

1. **Write down two dates:** the date on the order and the date you actually received it. Add 60 days to each. File well before the earlier one.
2. **Read the numbers.** The order must show the CAD's value and the value the board set (§41.47(c)). The board cannot set a value above the CAD's value unless you asked for it (§41.47(c-2)).
3. **Check what you would actually save.** For a homestead under the 10% cap, tax is computed on the *appraised* (capped) value, which can sit below market value. Lowering market value saves tax only when it falls below the capped amount (§23.23(a)). Work from the notice's capped number, not the market number.
4. **Estimate the yearly saving:** (value you can support - the ARB value) x (combined tax rate of your taxing units, as a decimal). Example with made-up numbers: ARB value $450,000, supportable value $410,000, combined rate 2.0% gives about $800 a year.
5. **Remember the carry-forward.** Once a value is lowered through a protest, arbitration, or suit, the CAD can raise it next year only with clear and convincing evidence (§23.01(e)). The saving usually repeats.
6. **Check your tax payments.** Every route requires you to pay the right amount before the delinquency date (taxes are delinquent if not paid before February 1, so pay by January 31 for bills mailed by January 10; see section 7). A delinquent account loses the appeal.
7. **Choose one route** from the table and calendar its deadline.

## 3. Regular binding arbitration

### Eligibility checklist (all must be true)

- The ARB decided a protest under §41.41(a)(1) (value) or (a)(2) (unequal appraisal) (§41A.01).
- The property is your **residence homestead** (any value), or the value in the ARB order is **$5 million or less**.
- Your taxes on the property are **not delinquent** (§41A.10(b)).
- You have **not sued in district court** on the same value, and have no signed settlement agreement with the CAD on it (§41A.03(b); Comptroller dismissal list).
- You file with the **Comptroller** (not the CAD or ARB) within **60 days after you receive the order** (§41A.03(a)). Missing any filing requirement waives the right (§41A.03(b)).

### Steps

1. **Pick the filing method.** Online through texas.gov is required if you use an agent or representative; pay by card or ACH. An owner without a representative may mail **Form AP-219** with a check or money order payable to the Texas Comptroller of Public Accounts, mailed to: Comptroller of Public Accounts, Attn: Regular Binding Arbitration, 111 E. 17th St., 4th floor, Austin, TX 78701 (address printed on Form AP-219, Rev. 7-26).
2. **State your opinion of value on the request.** The form asks for a short reason and your opinion of value (§41A.04). **That number is the yardstick for who wins** (see the table below), so give a figure your evidence can support.
3. **Pay the deposit** with the request.

| Property | Order value | Deposit | Arbitrator fee cap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Residence homestead | $500,000 or less | $450 | $400 |
| Residence homestead | over $500,000 | $500 | $450 |
| Not homestead | $1 million or less | $500 | $450 |
| Not homestead | over $1M to $2M | $800 | $750 |
| Not homestead | over $2M to $3M | $1,050 | $1,000 |
| Not homestead | over $3M to $5M | $1,550 | $1,500 |

(§41A.03(a)(2), §41A.06(b)(4); Comptroller schedule.) The Comptroller keeps **$50** of the deposit for administrative costs (§41A.05(b)).

4. **Cure defects if told.** The Comptroller cannot reject a request without written notice and 15 days to cure, even if the 60 days have passed (§41A.05(c)-(d)).
5. **Settlement window.** The Comptroller's page describes a 45-day period after processing during which you and the CAD can settle; you can withdraw in that period for a refund of the deposit minus $50 (online, or paper **Form 50-830**). The 45-day figure is Comptroller guidance, not a section I found in the statute.
6. **Arbitrator appointed and hearing set.** The arbitrator must reside in Texas and cannot have represented anyone for pay before that CAD, or worked for it or its ARB, in the last two years (§41A.07(e)-(f)). You may ask for an arbitrator who lives in the property's county or one who lives outside it (§41A.07(h)). The hearing follows Civil Practice and Remedies Code ch. 171 notice rules (§41A.08(a)). You may represent yourself or use an attorney, licensed broker or appraiser, registered property tax consultant, or CPA (§41A.08(b)).
7. **Award within 20 days after the hearing ends** (§41A.09(a)). It states a value and may include any remedy a court could order under ch. 42 on a value dispute (§41A.09(b)).
8. **The CAD corrects the roll.** If the arbitrator's value is lower than the ARB's, the chief appraiser must change it (§41A.09(c)(3), (d)(2)). A refund of any overpaid tax is due within 60 days, with 12% interest if late (§41A.10(a)).

### Who pays

| Outcome | Your deposit | Arbitrator fee paid by |
|---|---|---|
| Award value is **nearer your stated opinion** than the ARB value | Refunded, less $50 | The appraisal district |
| Award value is **not nearer** your opinion | Fee comes out of the deposit; the rest is refunded, less $50 | You (from the deposit) |
| You withdraw during the settlement window | Refunded, less $50 | Nobody |
| Withdraw after an arbitrator accepts | Arbitrator may charge up to the full fee; rest refunded, less $50 | You |
| Dismissed because your taxes were delinquent | Refunded, less $50 | Nobody |

(§41A.09(c)-(d), §41A.10(b); Comptroller arbitration page.) Worst realistic case on a $450 homestead deposit: you lose all $450. Best case: you lose $50.

### Evidence and strategy

- You can use the same comparable-sales and unequal-appraisal evidence you used at the ARB. The chapter does not say who carries the burden of proof; do not assume the CAD does.
- The arbitrator can apply the same unequal-appraisal remedies a court can (§41A.09(b)(2)).
- The award is final. It "may not be appealed" except on the narrow grounds in Civil Practice and Remedies Code §171.088 (§41A.09(b)(4)).

### When it is worth it

Compare the yearly saving to the worst-case cost. Using the made-up figures from section 2: a $40,000 value gap at a 2% combined rate is about $800 a year, against a worst-case cost of $450 and a best-case cost of $50. Because a lowered value is sticky (§23.01(e)), the saving usually continues. Arbitration is a poor fit when the gap is a few thousand dollars, when the capped appraised value is already below the value you would win, or when the real dispute is an exemption.

## 4. Limited binding arbitration (when the ARB broke a procedural rule)

Use this only for a procedural failure after you have filed a protest. The listed failures (§41A.015(a)):

- not following the ARB's own adopted hearing procedures;
- not scheduling a hearing as §41.45 requires;
- not delivering the information you requested under §41.461;
- not letting you offer evidence, question witnesses, or argue (§41.66(b));
- not setting a time and date certain, or not postponing when the hearing starts over two hours late (§41.66(i));
- not scheduling same-day hearings you asked for on multiple properties (§41.66(j));
- using evidence you requested but did not get at least 14 days before the hearing (§41.67(d)).

**Steps and clocks**

1. Within **5 business days** of the day the ARB or chief appraiser was required to comply, send written notice by **certified mail, return receipt requested**, to the ARB chairman, the chief appraiser, and the CAD's taxpayer liaison officer (§41A.015(b)(1)).
2. They have **10 days** to confirm in writing that they will comply or cure (§41A.015(b)(2)).
3. If they do not, file the request with the Comptroller on **days 11 through 30** after you delivered the notice (**Form AP-241**), with a deposit of **$450** (homestead valued at $500,000 or less) or **$550** (other) (§41A.015(d)-(e)).
4. A licensed attorney arbitrator (fee cap $400 for a homestead valued at $500,000 or less, $500 otherwise) decides. The award is final, orders the ARB or CAD to comply or to rescind its order and hold a new hearing, and cannot be appealed (§41A.015(j)).
5. If you win, your deposit is refunded less $50 and the CAD pays the arbitrator. If you lose, the fee comes out of your deposit (§41A.015(k)-(l)).
6. It does not take away your right to appeal the value afterward (§41A.015(n)). A failure on one of these points is not otherwise a ground to postpone a hearing (§41A.015(c)).

Cheaper alternatives for a procedural problem: ask the ARB for a postponement (§41.45(e), §41.66(h)), or complain to the taxpayer liaison officer (§41.66(q)).

## 5. SOAH (State Office of Administrative Hearings)

**Who qualifies:** value or unequal-appraisal protest (§41.41(a)(1)-(2)); the **ARB-ordered value is over $1 million**; the property is real or personal property **other than industrial property** (Gov't Code §2003.901, §2003.904). A homeowner with a home worth $1 million or less cannot use SOAH. They can use binding arbitration (homestead of any value) or court.

**Steps and clocks**

1. File the **Notice of Appeal by Property Owner** with the **chief appraiser** within **30 days** after you receive the ARB order (§2003.906(a-1)). The form is on the SOAH site.
2. Pay a **$1,500 deposit**, payable to SOAH, to the chief appraiser within **90 days** after you receive the order. If you miss it, SOAH dismisses the appeal **and you lose the right to use SOAH in any later tax year** (§2003.906(a-2), (a-3)).
3. The ALJ sets a hearing in the listed city nearest the property: Amarillo, Austin, Beaumont, Corpus Christi, El Paso, Fort Worth, Houston, Lubbock, Lufkin, McAllen, Midland, San Antonio, Tyler, or Wichita Falls (§2003.902, §2003.909).
4. The hearing is a trial de novo. The Texas Rules of Evidence and Gov't Code ch. 2001 do not apply, but the ALJ can weigh hearsay accordingly. Discovery is limited to exchanging the documents each side will rely on (§2003.910). You may represent yourself or use an attorney, CPA, registered property tax consultant, or other person (§2003.911).
5. The ALJ issues a written determination within **30 days** after the hearing (§2003.912(a)). It may include any remedy a court could order, including attorney's fees under §42.29.
6. Costs: if the ALJ's value is **nearer your stated opinion**, your deposit is refunded and the appraisal district pays the costs. If **not nearer**, SOAH keeps the deposit and you pay any costs above it (§2003.912(c)-(d)). If you settle before the hearing, the deposit comes back less the filing fee (§2003.906(a-2)); SOAH's page puts that fee at $300. SOAH also says ALJ time is billed at **$165 an hour** and cases that reach a determination average about **$6,500** (SOAH figures; they can change).
7. Pay the tax on the undisputed value before delinquency. Delinquent taxes get the appeal dismissed with prejudice and SOAH keeps the fee (§2003.913).

**Worth it?** Only when the dollars are large. The deposit is $1,500 and an adverse result can cost several thousand dollars more, so the saving needs to be well above that.

## 6. District court

**When to choose it:** large stakes, a legal question (for example an exemption denial), a property above the arbitration limits, or a case the owner wants a jury to hear. Arbitration costs a $450 or $500 deposit; a court case usually needs a lawyer.

**Steps and clocks**

1. **Pay first.** Before the delinquency date, pay the **lesser of**: tax on the undisputed value, tax under the ARB order, or last year's tax (§42.08(b)). Pay more if you like; it counts as paid under protest. If you cannot afford it, you may file an oath of inability and ask the court to excuse prepayment (§42.08(d)). If your unit offers split payment, half before December 1 and half before July 1 satisfies it (§42.08(c)). Miss it and you **forfeit** the right to a final decision.
2. **File a petition for review** in the district court of the county where the ARB sits, within **60 days after you received notice that a final order was entered**; you may file any time after the hearing and before the 60 days run out. A late petition bars the appeal (§42.21(a), §42.22). The owner does not file a separate notice of appeal (§42.06(a)).
3. **Sue the appraisal district, not the ARB** (§42.21(b)). Serve the chief appraiser or another officer or employee at the appraisal office (§42.21(d)).
4. **Trial de novo.** The judge or jury decides value fresh; the ARB's decision is not admissible to prove the facts (§42.23(a)-(c)). Any party may demand a jury.
5. **Remedies.** Excessive appraisal (§42.25) or unequal appraisal (§42.26). For unequal appraisal the court grants relief if your appraisal ratio is at least **10% above** the median of a reasonable and representative sample or of similarly situated properties, **or** your value exceeds the median appraised value of a reasonable number of adjusted comparables, with no 10% buffer on the comparables test (§42.26(a)). For a capped homestead the comparison uses market value (§42.26(d)).
6. **Settlement tools.** On motion the court sends the case to non-binding arbitration (§42.225) or mediation (§42.226); either side can ask for settlement talks (§42.227). The CAD may not require you to waive rights as a condition of settlement talks (§42.227(e)).
7. **Discovery limits added in 2025:** a court may not order discovery unless a party asks for it, and may not set expert-witness deadlines earlier than the Texas Rules of Civil Procedure allow unless the parties agree (§42.23(j)-(k), HB 1533, eff. 2025-09-01).
8. **Roll correction and refund.** After a final judgment the chief appraiser corrects the roll within 45 days (§42.41). The taxing unit refunds the overpayment with 9.5% interest from the delinquency date (you may waive the interest) (§42.43(b)), and the refund carries 12% interest if it is not made within 60 days (§42.43(c), SB 850).

**Costs and fees**

- The court may tax all or part of the costs to any party (§42.07).
- If you win under §42.25 or §42.26, on a §25.25 motion, or on a denial of an exemption under §11.17, 11.22, 11.23, 11.231 or 11.24, the court **may** award reasonable attorney's fees up to the greater of **$15,000** or **20%** of the tax reduction, and never more than the lesser of **$100,000** or the total reduction (§42.29). A denial of the ordinary homestead exemption under §11.13 is not on that list.
- Filing fees, service costs, and expert costs vary by county and case and were not researched here. Ask the district clerk.

**Lawyer or not:** you may represent yourself in a suit, but pleadings, discovery, and expert designations are strict. Treat court as a lawyer's route unless the dispute is small and simple.

## 7. Other doors when the protest window or the ARB route is closed

| Situation | Tool | Deadline | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wrong data, duplicate appraisal, nonexistent property, or wrong owner on a past roll | Motion to correct the roll (§25.25(c)) | Any of the **5 preceding tax years** | Heard by the ARB; if denied, sue to compel within 60 days (§25.25(g)) |
| Current-year value more than **one-fourth** too high (homestead) or **one-third** (other property) because of an error | Motion to correct (§25.25(d)); **Form 50-869** for homestead | Before the taxes are delinquent | Owner pays a 10% late-correction penalty on the corrected tax. Not allowed if the ARB already decided a protest on the merits or the value was set by agreement (§25.25(d-1)). |
| Homestead exemption never claimed or denied | Late application (§11.431) | Within 2 years after the delinquency date | The collector deducts or refunds the tax on the exempted amount |
| Disabled-veteran exemption never claimed | Late application (§11.439) | Within 5 years after the delinquency date (§11.22 for veteran or survivor; §11.131/§11.132 for the veteran's own homestead) | Same refund rule |
| Never got a required notice | Protest the failure to notify (§41.411) | Before taxes are delinquent | The ARB then decides any other ground too |
| Protest deadline missed | Late protest for good cause (§41.44(b)) | Before the ARB approves the records (about July 20) | See `deadlines.md` |

The delinquency date is February 1 of the year after the tax year (taxes are due on receipt of the bill and delinquent if not paid before February 1), later if the bill was mailed after January 10 (§31.02(a), §31.04(a)). Split-payment units have a December 1 / July 1 schedule (§31.03).

## 8. Help that costs nothing

- **Comptroller, Property Tax Assistance Division:** 800-252-9121 or 512-305-9999 (press 3 for general property tax questions, press 2 for arbitration). General information: ptad.cpa@cpa.texas.gov. The Comptroller cannot give legal advice and does not advise on a matter it knows is the subject of an ARB protest.
- **Taxpayer Assistance Pamphlet** (Comptroller publication 96-295) explains remedies and hearing preparation.
- **CAD taxpayer liaison officer** handles complaints about procedure and non-appraisal disputes (§41.66(q)); the Comptroller also collects ARB surveys (Form 50-823).
- **Comptroller county directory** lists each CAD's contact details.
