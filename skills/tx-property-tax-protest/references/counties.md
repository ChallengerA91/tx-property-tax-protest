The deadline and filing instructions printed on your appraisal notice always control. In `counties.json`, `null` means not confirmed, never that something does not exist.

## Notes that change how you file

- **Harris**: Online filing is keyed to the iFile number printed on the notice. Protest mail goes to P.O. Box 922004 (HCAD prints other P.O. boxes elsewhere).
- **Dallas**: uFile is opened from inside the property's account page and needs the PIN from the notice. DCAD says its ARB does not accept protests by fax or e-mail.
- **Tarrant**: 2026 change: protests are filed on a new True Prodigy portal that needs a new account. The older tad.org login is still used for informal value negotiation.
- **Bexar**: Property search now links to hgo.harrisgovern.com/bexar. One BCAD page prints a typo phone (210-224-2432); 210-242-2432 is correct.
- **Travis**: Property search and e-file moved to the Prodigy platform. TCAD says it no longer accepts fax.
- **Cameron**: No online protest portal confirmed: the CAD describes written protests by mail, fax, e-mail or in person only.
- **Bell**: Official name is 'Tax Appraisal District of Bell County'. It publishes protest@bellcad.org for protests only; 254-939-5841 is the Belton headquarters (Killeen and Temple offices have their own lines).
- **Nueces**: Online protest moved to portal.nuecescad.net. The homepage 'Online Appeals' link still points to the old site. Domain is nuecescad.net (ncadistrict.com redirects).
- **Lubbock**: Online protest is launched from the 'Online Protest' link on a property's Details screen in property search.
- **Webb**: Partly confirmed. Several Webb CAD procedure pages are download-only, so in-person filing rests on a secondary source.
- **Smith**: Only the online protest system is described on the CAD's site; call before relying on mail, fax or e-mail.
- **Ellis**: Only online filing is described on the CAD's site; call before relying on mail, fax or e-mail.

## If your county is not listed

Use the Texas Comptroller's county directory, <https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/county-directory/>. Select your county from the list of 254 to see the appraisal district's mailing address, street address, phone and fax, and the taxing units it serves. The districts report this information themselves, so confirm the phone number and the protest filing method on the district's own website, and check your appraisal notice, which names your district and says how to file. Look the property up by street address in the district's property search before assuming: some cities are served by a district in another county, and a property split by a county line gets a notice from each district.
